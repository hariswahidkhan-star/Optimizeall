The finding that shapes the architecture

The workbook forbids automating the single highest-value activity it describes. Golden Rule 5 — “No bulk sending, no automation tools, no bots, no scraping” — is repeated in the LinkedIn Playbook (step 3), QA & Compliance check 5, and Growth Playbook technique 15. LinkedIn also publishes no API for connection requests, personal messages or comments.

This is not an obstacle to design around. It is the design constraint. The outreach lane is therefore specified as prepare → verify → approve → human executes → confirm → measure, and the platform’s value there is the research, qualification, personalisation, sequencing, compliance and measurement — roughly 90% of the labour, and all of the leverage.

Identified AI Agent Workforce

Twenty-four agents in six divisions — not one giant agent, and not one per worksheet. Each gets a full formal definition in Phase 5; Phase 1 fixes the roster, boundaries and permissions, which is what needs settling now.

Division 0 — Command
ORCH
Chief Growth Orchestrator

Reads goals, KPIs and value ranks; sets today’s priorities; allocates against capacity; resolves dependencies; escalates. Cannot execute, approve, or change policy.

OPS
Scheduler & Operations

Owns the business calendar, recurring jobs, missed-job recovery, retries, dependencies, priority queues, concurrency and integration-health gating.

Division 1 — Intelligence
MKT
Market & Competitor Intelligence

Competitor profiles, industry and AI-in-project-controls developments, Gulf market signals, opportunity discovery. Every finding dated and cited.

LEAD
Lead Intelligence

Discovers and qualifies against the ICP, enriches, scores, deduplicates. Read-only against people data; no scraping connector, ever.

SEO
Search Optimisation

Keyword re-verification, cluster health, internal linking, technical SEO, ranking analysis, typed opportunity scoring.

AEO
Answer-Engine & Entity Authority

Monthly citation audit, entity-graph parity, crawler allow-list, schema, registry submissions.

ANA
Analytics & Attribution

Collects GA4, Search Console, Clarity and platform metrics; computes KPIs with lineage; never fabricates attribution.

Division 2 — Content
CSTRAT
Content Strategy

Chooses topic, pillar, cluster, campaign, audience, funnel stage, format, channel and CTA from the plan and performance memory.

CWRITE
Content Writer

Drafts from the Article Bank brief and its prompt, with citations, British English, and the brief’s word band.

CQA
Brand & Editorial QA

Voice, terminology, duplication, grammar, CTA quality, audience fit, structure. Also the second pair of eyes for four-eyes review.

PUB
Publishing & Syndication

Executes approved publication through official APIs only; enforces canonical rules and own-site-first ordering; idempotent.

Division 3 — Relationships
OUT
Outreach Composer

Composes only from approved templates; enforces character limits; requires a cited personal line.

PART
Partnership

Discovers, scores, stage-manages, prepares proposals, tracks next actions.

PR
PR & Media

Journalist requests, contributed articles, podcast pitches, press materials, expo and speaker applications.

COMM
Community

Drafts genuinely useful answers; enforces “useful without the link”; never posts.

EVT
Events & Webinars

Topic selection, speaker invitations, listings, promotion plan, post-event content plan.

LINK
Link Building

Competitor backlink gap, unlinked-mention reclaim, resource-page and broken-link prospecting.

DIR
Direct Channels

Email, WhatsApp, Telegram and SMS campaign preparation with consent and frequency enforcement.

Division 4 — Governance
COMP
Compliance & Safety

Pre-execution check of every external action against Golden Rules, QA checks, platform policy, suppression, consent, frequency caps and claim rules.

DQ
Data Health

The ten Dashboard checks as continuous validation, plus reconciliation against systems of record.

COST
Cost & Budget

Tracks model, API and channel spend; enforces caps; recommends downgrades that do not degrade quality.

Division 5 — Memory & reporting
KNOW
Knowledge

Ingests and versions the workbook, brand documents and policies; maintains six memory layers; serves retrieval with citations and permissions.

EXP
Experimentation

Registers hypotheses, baselines and guardrail metrics; applies the workbook’s own significance guard; never over-claims.

EXEC
Executive Reporting & Executive AI

Daily brief, end-of-day report, weekly review, and the natural-language interface answering only from system records, with citations.

Agent designs explicitly rejected

RejectedWhy
A “LinkedIn Agent” performing connects, DMs or commentsGolden Rule 5, QA check 5, Playbook step 3 and technique 15 all forbid it; LinkedIn’s terms forbid it; no API exists. Replaced by prepare → approve → human sends.
An auto-poster that publishes because a clock struckThe Content Scheduler is a commitment tracker, not a trigger. Publishing is the tail of QA, compliance and approval.

Software Requirements Specification

Sections 1–36. Every requirement is testable. Where the workbook does not determine an answer, the requirement says so and the item appears in the gaps register rather than being invented.

1 · Executive summary

PCI AI runs a growth department through a 42-sheet workbook that is, in substance, a complete operating model. It works, but it does not scale: it holds about eight working days of outreach rows at full-team volume, it cannot enforce its own rules, it cannot execute anything, and it depends on people remembering to log work the same day.

This specification defines a platform in which 24 specialised agents perform the research, drafting, analysis and coordination the workbook describes, while deterministic software owns identity, scheduling, permissions, state, budgets, approvals, integrations, compliance and audit.

Version 1 succeeds when the owner opens the platform in the morning and finds the workforce has already reviewed yesterday’s performance, produced today’s plan against the value-ranked objectives, run permitted research, drafted the day’s content and outreach, prepared everything needing a decision in one inbox, executed the authorised low-risk work, measured results, and recorded every step in an audit trail that answers why did the system do this?

2 · Business context

PCI AI issues three credentials plus an honorary certification, across five domains and two adjacent properties. Its buyers are project controls managers, planners, cost engineers, estimators, PMO leads and their employers, concentrated in the UK, US, Gulf, India and Australia.

Its position is contested at the most basic level — a competitor training provider currently owns PCI’s own name in Google, and the established bodies own the head terms. The workbook’s answer is compounding authority plus a direct outreach motion.

The commercial risk the workbook is most alert to is reputational: overstating a credential to a senior professional. Its first Golden Rule, several QA checks, the compliance component of the Employee Score, and the exact wording of every approved template all exist to prevent it. Any system generating outreach or public content for PCI inherits that as its primary design constraint.

Architecture Decisions Requiring Approval

Fourteen decisions. None should be implemented before approval.

D-01

Overall backend shape

Option A
Single modular monolith in one language
Option B
Modular monolith for platform and domain, plus a separate agent-runtime service
Option C
Microservices per bounded context
Recommend
Option B
Reasoning
The deterministic half — identity, RBAC, scheduling, approvals, policy, integrations, audit, KPIs — is ordinary transactional software that benefits from one database and one deployment. The AI half has a different dependency profile, failure mode and scaling curve. Splitting exactly once, along that seam, buys isolation without distributed-systems tax.
Trade-offs
Two deployables and one internal contract to version. C is rejected: at one organisation with one administrator, microservices multiply operational burden without a scaling need.
D-02

Language split

Option A
.NET 9 platform plus Python/FastAPI agent runtime
Option B
Python/FastAPI end to end
Recommend
Option A, conditional on team skills
Reasoning
.NET gives strong typing, migrations and a mature security stack where correctness matters most; Python is where the AI ecosystem actually lives.
Trade-offs
Two ecosystems and two CI pipelines. If your team is Python-only, Option B is the better decision — a single-language team shipping reliably beats an optimal split nobody can maintain. This is a people question, and I need the answer before Phase 2.
D-03

Workflow orchestration

Option A
Temporal
Option B
Hangfire, Quartz or Celery plus a custom state machine
Option C
Build a workflow engine
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
These workflows wait on humans for hours or days and must survive restarts without duplicating external actions. That is durable execution. Timers, approval waits, mid-flight versioning, retries and visibility all come free; building them on a job queue means reinventing all of it.
Trade-offs
Real operational weight for one organisation. Option B is materially cheaper and defensible for an MVP — the cost is that persisted state and safe resume become code you own. Option C is rejected outright.
D-04

Queueing and messaging

Option A
PostgreSQL transactional outbox plus Temporal task queues; Redis for cache and rate limiting only
Option B
A message broker as the event backbone
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
Domain events must publish atomically with the state change that caused them, or idempotent consumers become guesswork. An outbox gives that for free. At hundreds to low thousands of tasks a day, a broker adds a moving part without adding throughput anyone needs.
Trade-offs
Polling latency measured in seconds — irrelevant here. Revisit at multi-tenant scale.
D-05

Vector search

Option A
pgvector in the primary database
Option B
A dedicated vector database
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
Permission-scoped and version-aware retrieval means joining chunks to documents, organisations, roles and versions. In pgvector that is a WHERE clause; in a separate store it is a distributed consistency problem. At ≤5M chunks it is comfortably sufficient.
Trade-offs
Index build time and memory pressure at large scale; mitigate with a read replica.
D-06

Authentication provider

Option A
Microsoft Entra ID (OIDC)
Option B
A hosted identity vendor
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
The workbook already lives in SharePoint or OneDrive, so the organisation is on Microsoft identity. Reusing it gives MFA, conditional access and joiner/leaver control immediately — and joiner/leaver control is an explicit workbook concern.
Trade-offs
Ties the MVP to Microsoft identity; a SaaS phase later needs a broker. Never build identity.
D-07

Deployment target

Option A
Docker Compose on a managed VM, then a container service
Option B
Kubernetes from day one
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
One organisation, one administrator, predictable load. Making local and production the same shape reduces the number of things that can break in only one environment.
Trade-offs
A later migration if the SaaS phase arrives — cheap while services stay stateless and configuration externalised, both already required.
D-09

Model providers

Option A
A primary and a secondary provider plus embeddings, all behind the gateway
Option B
A single provider
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
Two approved providers is the minimum that makes fallback real. Agent configuration should reference model classes, not names, so upgrades are configuration changes.
Trade-offs
Two vendor relationships, two rate-limit regimes, and data-classification routing rules to maintain.
D-10

Agent orchestration framework

Option A
Direct provider SDKs plus platform-owned orchestration
Option B
An agent framework
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
Business workflows must be owned by PCI AI, not trapped in a framework. The durable engine already owns control flow; a second orchestration layer with its own opinions about state, retries and memory would compete with it.
Trade-offs
More code for tool loops and output validation — which is exactly where the audit and policy hooks must live anyway.
D-11

Social scheduling approach

Option A
Native platform schedulers plus the workbook’s recommended free stack
Option B
Build direct API publishing for every social platform
Recommend
Option A for v1; direct API only for the website, later the LinkedIn Company Page and YouTube
Reasoning
Most platforms schedule natively with real limits. Building ten write connectors would consume the entire MVP budget automating the least valuable part of the chain. The valuable part is deciding what to publish and proving it is safe.
Trade-offs
Coverage tracking depends partly on human confirmation until direct connectors exist.
D-12

Multi-tenancy strategy

Option A
Single database, organisation key on every row, row-level security
Option B
Database per tenant
Recommend
Option A
Reasoning
Meets isolation and readiness requirements without operational multiplication, and makes cross-organisation queries impossible by construction rather than by developer discipline.
Trade-offs
Noisy-neighbour risk and a heavier migration story at scale; acceptable for one organisation plus a few future tenants.
D-13

CRM strategy

Option A
Build the lead engine in the platform, integrate a CRM later
Option B
Integrate a CRM from day one as the lead system of record
Recommend
Option A for MVP, with the model designed for B
Reasoning
No CRM is named in the workbook, and the changelog only says the lead log belongs in a CRM at sustained full-team volume. Building an integration to an unchosen product is not possible; building sync-ready contracts is.
Trade-offs
A future migration of lead ownership, mitigated by specifying the sync contract now.
